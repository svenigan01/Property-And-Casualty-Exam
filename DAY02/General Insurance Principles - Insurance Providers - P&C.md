# Property & Casualty Insurance - Practice Quiz & Answer Key (Part 2)

### Question 1
**A stock insurance company is owned by its:**

- [ ] a. board of directors
- [ ] b. members
- [ ] c. policyholders
- [x] **d. stockholders**

> **Explanation:** A stock insurance company is owned by stockholders who purchase shares of stock as an investment.

---

### Question 2
**Melanie sells an insurance policy from Outlier Insurance, which is not authorized to transact insurance in her state. Her client later files a claim that Outlier wrongfully refuses to pay. Melanie may be required to:**

- [ ] a. force Outlier to pay the claim
- [ ] b. help Outlier become an authorized insurer
- [x] **c. pay the amount of the unpaid claim**
- [ ] d. sue Outlier

> **Explanation:** A producer who sells coverage from an unauthorized entity may be liable for unpaid claims, subject to regulatory penalties, and even imprisoned for committing a felony.

---

### Question 3
**Which kind of insurer involves mutual agreements to indemnify the insureds from loss?**

- [ ] a. Lloyd's association
- [ ] b. Mutual insurer
- [x] **c. Reciprocal insurer**
- [ ] d. Stock insurer

> **Explanation:** Sometimes called reciprocal exchanges, a reciprocal insurer is an unincorporated group of members that insure each other by exchanging contracts of indemnity that obligate them to collectively pay any member's covered loss.

---

### Question 4
**A mutual insurance company is owned by its:**

- [ ] a. board of directors
- [ ] b. members
- [x] **c. policyholders**
- [ ] d. stockholders

> **Explanation:** A mutual insurance company is owned by the people and organizations that own insurance policies which the insurance company has issued.

### Question 1
**In a standard insurance sales transaction involving an applicant, a producer, and an insurance company, which party is the offeree?**

- [ ] a. applicant
- [ ] b. beneficiary
- [x] **c. insurer**
- [ ] d. producer

> **Explanation:** In the typical insurance transaction, the applicant (offeror) makes the offer to the insurance company (offeree) through a signed application plus a premium deposit.

---

### Question 2
**If the courts find an ambiguity in an insurance policy, they will interpret it in the policyholder's favor because insurance policies are:**

- [ ] a. aleatory contracts
- [x] **b. contracts of adhesion**
- [ ] c. indemnity contracts
- [ ] d. personal contracts

> **Explanation:** Insurance contracts are contracts of adhesion, and if the courts find that a contract of adhesion is ambiguous, they will interpret it in favor of the party that did not write the contract.

---

### Question 3
**The fact that insurance policy language is written by an insurance company and is nonnegotiable makes insurance policies:**

- [ ] a. aleatory contracts
- [ ] b. conditional contracts
- [x] **c. contracts of adhesion**
- [ ] d. unilateral contracts

> **Explanation:** Most insurance contracts are contracts of adhesion because the policy is drafted by the insurer and little if any negotiation is normally available with respect to the policy's wording.

---

- [ ] d. shred it for security reasons# Property & Casualty Insurance - Practice Quiz & Answer Key (Part 5)

### Question 1
**Evelyn, who lives alone, never locks the door to her apartment when she goes to work. This is an example of a:**

- [ ] a. Legal hazard
- [ ] b. Moral hazard
- [x] **c. Morale hazard**
- [ ] d. Physical hazard

> **Explanation:** Not locking a car door or a house door is a morale hazard because Evelyn's carelessness or indifference increases the likelihood of a theft.

---

### Question 2
**Collette lives in a home on the ocean that is insured for $20 million. Although her insurance company would not be able to cover a $20 million loss with its own resources, the insurer is able to write a single policy with a $20 million limit for Collette because the insurance company has:**

- [ ] a. A large deductible in its policy
- [ ] b. Competition from the California Department of Insurance
- [x] **c. Reinsurance**
- [ ] d. Secured credit

> **Explanation:** By purchasing reinsurance, the insurer transfers some of its risk to a reinsurance company, and that makes it possible for the insurer to handle this large account.

---

### Question 3
**Which of the following is NOT a cost associated with operating an insurance system?**

- [ ] a. Cost of capital investment
- [ ] b. Cost of labor
- [ ] c. Increased losses due to attitudinal hazards
- [x] **d. Reduced crime rates**

> **Explanation:** The costs associated with operating an insurance system include land, capital investment, and labor, as well as increased losses due to attitudinal and moral hazards. Reduced crime rates are not a direct cost of the system.

---

### Question 4
**Wilmer's auto insurance paid $2,000 of the $2,500 it will cost to repair the collision damage to Wilmer's car. It did not pay the remaining $500 because Wilmer's policy has a(n):**

- [ ] a. $2,000 limit
- [ ] b. Coinsurance clause
- [x] **c. Deductible**
- [ ] d. Self-funding clause

> **Explanation:** Wilmer's policy has a $500 collision deductible, which means the insurer will not pay $500 of a collision loss.

### Question 1
**Colossal Casualty Insurance Company, an admitted insurance company in California, has appointed Winfred, who has a California insurance agent's license, to sell Colossal Casualty insurance policies. When he is engaged in providing insurance for Justine, Winfred represents:**

- [ ] a. Both Colossal Casualty and Justine.
- [x] **b. Colossal Casualty.**
- [ ] c. Justine.
- [ ] d. The California Department of Insurance.

> **Explanation:** A licensed insurance agent (Winfred) represents the admitted insurance company (Colossal) that has formally appointed him as its agent.

---

### Question 2
**Colossal Casualty Insurance Company, which is incorporated in Pennsylvania and has its home office in Philadelphia, is an admitted insurer in California. California regulators would categorize Colossal Casualty as what kind of insurer?**

- [ ] a. Alien
- [ ] b. Domestic
- [x] **c. Foreign**
- [ ] d. Unregulated

> **Explanation:** An admitted insurer doing business in a state other than the one where it is domiciled is a foreign insurer.

---

### Question 3
**Georgina's insurance company notifies her that it will not renew her current policy when it expires because her property no longer meets the insurer's underwriting criteria. It appears that Georgina's insurer is engaging in a practice known as:**

- [x] **a. Post-selection.**
- [ ] b. Pre-selection.
- [ ] c. Pretext interview.
- [ ] d. Unfair discrimination.

> **Explanation:** Reviewing existing insurance contracts like Georgina's and nonrenewing coverage on policyholders who are no longer found to be acceptable is an example of post-selection.

---

### Question 4
**Three different insurance companies licensed in California have rejected Ferris's application for liability insurance on his amusement park. However, Ferris might still be able to get the coverage he needs from a(n):**

- [ ] a. Alien insurer.
- [ ] b. Domestic insurer.
- [ ] c. Foreign insurer.
- [x] **d. Nonadmitted insurer.**

> **Explanation:** A California consumer is legally permitted to buy excess and surplus lines insurance from a nonadmitted insurer, who might or might not be domiciled in California, when the desired coverage is not available from an admitted insurer.

### Question 1
**Which of these is NOT considered a fraudulent act with respect to insurance transactions in California?**

- [ ] a. knowingly exaggerating facts about a loss to obtain a larger claim settlement
- [ ] b. materially misrepresenting facts to discourage a party from exercising its rights
- [x] **c. paying a claim before the insurer has completed its investigation of the loss**
- [ ] d. withholding information from an insured that would increase the amount of the claim settlement

> **Explanation:** An insurer that pays a claim before completing its investigation of the loss does not commit fraud.

---

### Question 2
**How long must agents keep their business records available for inspection by the California Insurance Commissioner?**

- [ ] a. 10 years
- [ ] b. 3 years
- [x] **c. 5 years**
- [ ] d. 7 years

> **Explanation:** Agents must keep their business records for at least five years and make them available to the Commissioner for inspection.

---

### Question 3
**The parties to an insurance contract include the:**

- [ ] a. insurer and beneficiary
- [ ] b. insurer and claimant
- [x] **c. insurer and policyowner**
- [ ] d. insurer, policyowner, and beneficiary

> **Explanation:** An insurance contract is an agreement between the insurer and the policyowner. Neither the beneficiary nor the claimant is a party.

---

### Question 4
**An advantage that an admitted insurer has over an unadmitted insurer in California is the ability to:**

- [ ] a. comply with state laws and regulations
- [ ] b. license its agents in California
- [ ] c. settle claims from California residents
- [x] **d. transact insurance business in California**

> **Explanation:** An admitted insurer is entitled and certified to transact insurance business in California.

# Property & Casualty Insurance - Practice Quiz & Answer Key (Part 8)

### Question 1
**All of the following are true of California's "Shine the Light" Law EXCEPT:**

- [ ] a. Businesses with less than 20 employees are exempt
- [ ] b. The for-profit business does not have to be located in California but must have a customer who resides in California.
- [x] **c. The for-profit business must be located in California and have customers who reside in California.**
- [ ] d. The law requires businesses to establish a designated contact point.

> **Explanation:** The law requires businesses to establish a designated contact point, the law does not apply to businesses with less than 20 employees, and the business does not have to be located in California but must have customers who reside in California.

---

### Question 2
**Which California privacy act gives more control to consumers over their personal information collected by businesses?**

- [x] **a. California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)**
- [ ] b. California's "Shine the Light" Law
- [ ] c. Fair Credit Reporting Act
- [ ] d. Insurance Information and Privacy Protection Act

> **Explanation:** Under the California Consumer Privacy Act, California residents may ask businesses to disclose what personal information they have about themselves and what they do with that information, to delete personal information and not to sell personal information.

---

### Question 3
**California's "Shine the Light" Law would apply to which of the following businesses?**

- [x] **a. An advertising firm with 55 employees**
- [ ] b. California non-profit organization
- [ ] c. Credit reporting bureau
- [ ] d. Credit union

> **Explanation:** Exceptions include businesses with less than 20 employees, federal financial institutions, non-profit organizations, political groups, providers of public real estate records, and credit reporting bureaus.

---

### Question 4
**Which of the following would NOT be considered personal information under the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)?**

- [ ] a. Fingerprints
- [ ] b. Internet browsing history
- [x] **c. Real estate license number**
- [ ] d. Receipts of products purchased

> **Explanation:** Fingerprints, records of products purchased, and internet browsing history are all considered personal information under the CCPA. Professional licenses are not considered personal information.
