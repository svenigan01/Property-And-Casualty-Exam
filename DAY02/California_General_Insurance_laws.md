
# Property & Casualty Insurance - California General Insurance laws - Basic Insurance Concepts and Principles


### Question 1
**In California, which one of the following does not qualify as insurance?**

- [ ] a. Casandra's homeowners policy
- [x] **b. Debbie's agreement to regularly clean a dining hall**
- [ ] c. Kelly's auto insurance policy
- [ ] d. Mason's workers compensation insurance

> **Explanation:** Debbie's agreement to regularly clean a dining hall is not a promise to pay for loss or damage arising from an uncertain event.

---

### Question 2
**Collette lives in a home on the ocean that is insured for $20 million. Although her insurance company would not be able to cover a $20 million loss with its own resources, the insurer is able to write a single policy with a $20 million limit for Collette because the insurance company has:**

- [ ] a. A large deductible in its policy.
- [ ] b. Competition from the California Department of Insurance.
- [x] **c. Reinsurance.**
- [ ] d. Secured credit.

> **Explanation:** By purchasing reinsurance, the insurer transfers some of its risk to a reinsurance company, and that makes it possible for the insurer to handle this large account.

---

### Question 3
**In his application for property insurance, Faustino stated that his California building had an operating automatic fire extinguishing system. He did not mention that the main valve to the system has been turned off due to a leak that was never repaired. If the insurer issues a policy and there is a fire in this building:**

- [ ] a. Faustino's insurer cannot deny the claim because his misrepresentation does not qualify as a warranty in California.
- [ ] b. Faustino's insurer cannot deny the claim, because in California whether a building has a sprinkler system has no bearing on the insurer's decision to insure it for a certain premium.
- [x] **c. Faustino's insurer may deny a claim because in California his misrepresentation qualifies as an implied warranty.**
- [ ] d. The warranty on Faustino's sprinkler system has apparently expired.

> **Explanation:** Faustino's false representation in this matter that would affect the insurer's decision to issue a policy may qualify as an implied warranty that gives the insurer grounds to deny a claim.

---

### Question 4
**Evelyn, who lives alone, never locks the door to her apartment when she goes to work. This is an example of a:**

- [ ] a. Legal hazard.
- [ ] b. Moral hazard.
- [x] **c. Morale Hazard.**
- [ ] d. Physical Hazard.

> **Explanation:** Not locking a car door or a house door is a morale hazard because Evelyn's carelessness or indifference increases the likelihood of a theft.

# Property & Casualty Insurance - California General Insurance laws - The Insurance Marketplace


### Question 1
**As a recently licensed California insurance agent, Alonzo realizes that he could make a mistake with an insurance policy that might cause an angry client to sue him. Therefore, Alonzo realizes it is important for him to carry:**

- [ ] a. A businessowners policy.
- [ ] b. A crime insurance policy.
- [x] **c. Errors and omissions insurance.**
- [ ] d. Life insurance.

> **Explanation:** Alonzo should carry errors and omissions insurance to protect himself from liability that might arise because he fails to fulfill his duties to either his customers or the insurer(s) he represents.

---

### Question 2
**Colossal Casualty Insurance Company, an admitted insurance company in California, has appointed Winfred, who has a California insurance agent's license, to sell Colossal Casualty insurance policies. When he is engaged in providing insurance for Justine, Winfred represents:**

- [ ] a. Both Colossal Casualty and Justine.
- [x] **b. Colossal Casualty.**
- [ ] c. Justine.
- [ ] d. The California Department of Insurance.

> **Explanation:** A licensed insurance agent (Winfred) represents the admitted insurance company (Colossal) that has formally appointed him as its agent.

---

### Question 3
**Colossal Casualty Insurance Company, which is incorporated in Pennsylvania and has its home office in Philadelphia, is an admitted insurer in California. California regulators would categorize Colossal Casualty as what kind of insurer?**

- [ ] a. Alien
- [ ] b. Domestic
- [x] **c. Foreign**
- [ ] d. Unregulated

> **Explanation:** An admitted insurer doing business in a state other than the one where it is domiciled is a foreign insurer.

---

### Question 4
**What is the role of the California Insurance Guarantee Association?**

- [ ] a. To ensure access to affordable insurance for all property owners.
- [ ] b. To protect insurance agents and brokers against policyholders who do not pay their premiums.
- [ ] c. To protect insurance companies against policyholders who do not pay their premiums.
- [x] **d. To protect policyholders against insolvent insurers.**

> **Explanation:** The California Insurance Guarantee Association, like other states' guaranty funds, protects policyholders against insolvent insurers.

# Property & Casualty Insurance - California General Insurance laws - State Insurance Regulation and Contract Law

### Question 1
**Which of these is NOT considered a fraudulent act with respect to insurance transactions in California?**

- [ ] a. knowingly exaggerating facts about a loss to obtain a larger claim settlement
- [ ] b. materially misrepresenting facts to discourage a party from exercising its rights
- [x] **c. paying a claim before the insurer has completed its investigation of the loss**
- [ ] d. withholding information from an insured that would increase the amount of the claim settlement

> **Explanation:** An insurer that pays a claim before completing its investigation of the loss does not commit fraud.

---

### Question 2
**California regulates the insurance business within its state lines. However, the federal government regulates insurance business that is associated with:**

- [ ] a. fiduciary bonds
- [ ] b. inland marine risks
- [x] **c. interstate commerce**
- [ ] d. professional liability

> **Explanation:** While the state principally regulates insurance business within its boundaries or transacted by its licensees, the business comes under federal jurisdiction when it affects or is associated with interstate commerce.

---

### Question 3
**Under federal law, those in the insurance industry who knowingly provide misleading material information to government regulators to influence their actions are subject to:**

- [ ] a. a fine alone
- [x] **b. a fine, a prison term, or both**
- [ ] c. a prison term alone
- [ ] d. a prison term and a fine

> **Explanation:** A person engaged in the insurance business who knowingly provides false material information to a government official or agency to influence the actions of the official or agency is subject to a fine, imprisonment for up to ten years, or both. However, if the false information compromised the solvency of an insurer, the person is subject to imprisonment for up to 15 years.

---

### Question 4
**How long must agents keep their business records available for inspection by the California Insurance Commissioner?**

- [ ] a. 10 years
- [ ] b. 3 years
- [x] **c. 5 years**
- [ ] d. 7 years

> **Explanation:** Agents must keep their business records for at least five years and make them available to the Commissioner for inspection.

# Property & Casualty Insurance - California General Insurance laws - California Privacy Laws

### Question 1
**All of the following are true of California's "Shine the Light" Law EXCEPT:**

- [ ] a. Businesses with less than 20 employees are exempt
- [ ] b. The for-profit business does not have to be located in California but must have a customer who resides in California.
- [x] **c. The for-profit business must be located in California and have customers who reside in California.**
- [ ] d. The law requires businesses to establish a designated contact point.

> **Explanation:** The law requires businesses to establish a designated contact point, the law does not apply to businesses with less than 20 employees, and the business does not have to be located in California but must have customers who reside in California.

---

### Question 2
**The California Consumer Privacy Act of 2018 (CCPA) gives California residents the right to do all of the following EXCEPT:**

- [ ] a. non-discrimination for exercising their CCPA rights
- [ ] b. opt-out of the sale of personal information
- [x] **c. request that a business delete all personal information ever collected from the consumer**
- [ ] d. request that the business disclose what personal information they have collected, used, or shared

> **Explanation:** Consumers may opt-out, request businesses to disclose personal information collected, used, or shared, and are entitled to non-discrimination for exercising their CCPA rights. Consumers have the right to request businesses to delete personal information, but many exceptions allow businesses to keep the personal information of their customers for the purpose of doing business with them.

---

### Question 3
**California's "Shine the Light" Law would apply to which of the following businesses?**

- [x] **a. an advertising firm with 55 employees**
- [ ] b. California non-profit organization
- [ ] c. credit reporting bureau
- [ ] d. credit union

> **Explanation:** Exceptions include businesses with less than 20 employees, federal financial institutions, non-profit organizations, political groups, providers of public real estate records, and credit reporting bureaus.

---

### Question 4
**Which California privacy act gives more control to consumers over their personal information collected by businesses?**

- [x] **a. California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)**
- [ ] b. California's "Shine the Light" Law
- [ ] c. Fair Credit Reporting Act
- [ ] d. Insurance Information and Privacy Protection Act

> **Explanation:** Under the California Consumer Privacy Act, California residents may ask businesses to disclose what personal information they have about themselves and what they do with that information, to delete personal information, and not to sell personal information.
