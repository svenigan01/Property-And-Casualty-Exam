

# Property & Casualty Insurance - Property and Casualty Insurance Basics - Property Insurance Basics

### Question 1
**Ken's house has an exterior layer of brick that covers wooden walls supported by wooden 2-by-4 studs. What type of construction does Ken's house have?**

- [ ] a. fire resistive
- [ ] b. frame
- [ ] c. masonry
- [x] **d. masonry veneer**

> **Explanation:** A building of masonry veneer construction has exterior walls of combustible construction with an outside veneer (layer) of brick or stone; frame construction behind the veneer surface supports the building.

---

### Question 2
**Which of the following items constitutes personal property?**

- [ ] a. a custom bookcase built into a house's den
- [ ] b. a house's French doors
- [x] **c. a house's refrigerator**
- [ ] d. the sink in a house's kitchen

> **Explanation:** The refrigerator is personal property. All other items are attached to the building and thus constitute real property.

---

### Question 3
**For an insurer to pay a property or casualty claim, when must the policyholder have an insurable interest in the insured risk?**

- [x] **a. at the time of the loss**
- [ ] b. only when the claim is submitted
- [ ] c. only when the insurance is purchased
- [ ] d. only when the policy is purchased or renewed

> **Explanation:** To receive the proceeds from a property or casualty policy, an insured must have an insurable interest in the insured risk at the time of the loss.

---

### Question 4
**Corinne purchased her home with a 10 percent down payment and a 90 percent mortgage from Sidney Bank. Why does Sidney Bank have an insurable interest in Corinne's house?**

- [ ] a. Corinne cannot afford to make mortgage payments on her own.
- [x] **b. The bank faces the possibility of a loss if Corinne's house is damaged or destroyed.**
- [ ] c. The mortgage documents Corinne signed make the bank a coinsured.
- [ ] d. The mortgage holder (bank) is responsible for paying insurance premiums on a mortgaged house.

> **Explanation:** The property owner and the mortgage holder both have an insurable interest in Corinne's home, because both will suffer a loss if the home is damaged.

# Property & Casualty Insurance - Property and Casualty Insurance Basics - Causes of Loss

### Question 1
**Which of the following is commonly listed as an excluded cause of loss in an open perils property insurance policy?**

- [ ] a. explosion
- [x] **b. flood**
- [ ] c. vandalism
- [ ] d. windstorm

> **Explanation:** An open (special) perils policy typically lists flood as an excluded peril, necessitating the need for flood insurance or a difference-in-conditions (D-I-C) policy to cover that risk.

---

### Question 2
**A house is struck by lightning during a thunderstorm, which starts a fire. The house stands in a floodplain and the basement fills with water from the storm. The homeowner files a claim on her homeowners insurance policy, which excludes losses from floods. What is true about her coverage?**

- [ ] a. It will cover only the loss from the fire.
- [x] **b. It will cover the loss.**
- [ ] c. It will not cover the loss.
- [ ] d. It will partially cover the loss from the fire and the flood.

> **Explanation:** Under the doctrine of concurrent causation, if a loss involves more than one peril, and at least one of the perils is covered by the policy, then the loss is covered.

---

### Question 3
**Elaine's property insurance policy lists and describes eight covered causes of loss. These causes of loss may also be referred to as:**

- [ ] a. moral hazards
- [ ] b. morale hazards
- [x] **c. perils**
- [ ] d. physical hazards

> **Explanation:** "Cause of loss" and "peril" mean exactly the same thing.

---

### Question 4
**With an open perils policy, which of the following statements correctly describes the burden of proof with respect to covering a loss?**

- [ ] a. To cover the loss, the policyholder has the burden of proof to show the loss was caused by a covered peril.
- [ ] b. To cover the loss, the policyholder has the burden of proof to show the loss was not caused by an excluded peril.
- [x] **c. To deny coverage, the insurer has the burden of proof to show the loss was caused by an excluded peril.**
- [ ] d. To deny coverage, the insurer has the burden of proof to show the loss was not caused by a covered peril.

> **Explanation:** With an open perils policy, the burden of proof lies with the insurer to show that a loss was caused by an excluded peril. If the insurer cannot prove the cause of a loss was an excluded peril, the loss is covered.

# Property & Casualty Insurance - Property and Casualty Insurance Basics - Liability Insurance Basics

### Question 1
**Bill's children have grown up and moved away, but the tree house that he built for them now draws other neighborhood children despite his handmade "No Trespassing" sign. If a neighbor's child is injured by falling from the tree house, Bill might be held liable for the child's injuries under what legal doctrine or principle?**

- [ ] a. absolute liability
- [x] **b. attractive nuisance**
- [ ] c. contributory negligence
- [ ] d. vicarious liability

> **Explanation:** Under the attractive nuisance doctrine, a party can be held liable for maintaining something on its property that is attractive to curious children even though the children are technically trespassers.

---

### Question 2
**Liability insurance basically involves:**

- [ ] a. contract law
- [ ] b. criminal law
- [ ] c. international law
- [x] **d. tort law**

> **Explanation:** Liability insurance involves tort law, which protects the rights of individuals from civil wrongs.

---

### Question 3
**Sally was seriously injured when she collided with Frank at an intersection. A court determined that the accident was 20 percent Sally's fault, so she was entitled to recover 80 percent of her damages from Frank. The defense was based on the doctrine of:**

- [ ] a. absolute liability
- [ ] b. assumption of risk
- [x] **c. comparative negligence**
- [ ] d. contributory negligence

> **Explanation:** Under the comparative negligence doctrine, the amount of the plaintiff's recovery is reduced by the portion of his or her fault. Because Sally was 20 percent at fault, the amount of damages that she would otherwise be entitled to recover is reduced by 20 percent.

---

### Question 4
**Which one of the following incidents is an example of vicarious liability?**

- [ ] a. Dominic is held responsible for food poisoning due to a pizza he delivered.
- [ ] b. Edie is held responsible for striking a pedestrian with her bicycle.
- [ ] c. Lilly is held responsible for her cat's injury to Lilly's guest.
- [x] **d. Rudy is held responsible for an accident caused by his employee.**

> **Explanation:** Vicarious liability occurs when an employer is held liable for damages resulting from the negligence of an employee who was acting in the course and scope of his or her employment.

# Property & Casualty Insurance - Property and Casualty Insurance Basics - The Underwriting and Rating Process

### Question 1
**Why does an underwriter guard against adverse selection?**

- [ ] a. To insure only persons who can afford the premiums
- [ ] b. To insure only persons with the least risk
- [x] **c. To minimize insuring persons with the most risk**
- [ ] d. To prevent premium inflation

> **Explanation:** An underwriter is responsible for guarding against adverse selection, which is the tendency of people at the most risk of loss to buy and maintain insurance.

---

### Question 2
**If a group of insureds are rated by class, they will:**

- [x] **a. be charged the same premium rate**
- [ ] b. be exposed to the same risks
- [ ] c. experience the same losses
- [ ] d. file the same claims

> **Explanation:** Insureds who share similar characteristics with others may be charged the same premium rate as a class.

---

### Question 3
**Before permitting Roofing Contractor to work on his roof, Delmar asks Roofing Contractor to give him a certificate of insurance, which serves as:**

- [ ] a. a record of Roofing Contractor's past losses
- [ ] b. evidence of Roofing Contractor's financial status
- [x] **c. evidence that Roofing Contractor has purchased certain types of insurance**
- [ ] d. temporary coverage until a policy is issued

> **Explanation:** A certificate of insurance is a document issued by or on behalf of an insurance company as evidence that a certain type of insurance coverage with specified limits has been purchased by the party that was required to provide the certificate.

---

### Question 4
**QBS Insurance Company uses class rating to determine the premiums for its auto insurance policies, which means that the rate for every insured driver is:**

- [ ] a. based on the underwriter's judgment of that individual applicant
- [ ] b. based solely on that driver's past loss experience.
- [x] **c. determined by placing the driver and vehicle into a class with other similar drivers and vehicles**
- [ ] d. developed by evaluating the vehicle's construction, protection, and other factors

> **Explanation:** With class rating, all insureds with similar characteristics are placed into the same rating class and are charged the same rates.

# Property & Casualty Insurance - Property and Casualty Insurance Basics - Policy Structure

### Question 1
**Exclusions from coverage will NOT:**

- [ ] a. Eliminate or reduce overlapping coverage
- [ ] b. Encourage loss prevention
- [x] **c. Increase the premium**
- [ ] d. Remove unnecessary coverage

> **Explanation:** Exclusions help to keep premiums reasonable by removing coverage that might otherwise apply.

---

### Question 2
**Property insurance policies covering buildings and contents commonly exclude all the following causes of loss EXCEPT:**

- [ ] a. earth movement
- [ ] b. leakage or seepage
- [x] **c. lightning**
- [ ] d. mechanical breakdown

> **Explanation:** Lightning is a covered peril in nearly all property insurance policies. However, earth movement, leakage and seepage, and mechanical breakdown are usually excluded.

---

### Question 3
**A statement in an auto insurance policy that reads: "We will pay damages for bodily injury or property damage for which any insured becomes legally responsible because of an auto accident" is from which standard policy provision?**

- [ ] a. conditions
- [ ] b. declaration
- [ ] c. definitions
- [x] **d. insuring agreement**

> **Explanation:** An insuring agreement, which might also be called an insuring clause, is a very broad statement that sets forth the insurer's basic promises under the policy.

---

### Question 4
**The declarations of his personal auto insurance policy lists Rich as the named insured. However, the policy's definition of "insured" indicates that drivers living in his household are also insured, which would seem to cover his wife, Alison, and their teenage son Johnny. When this policy uses the word "you," to whom is it referring?**

- [ ] a. Alison and Johnny
- [x] **b. Rich**
- [ ] c. Rich and Alison
- [ ] d. Rich, Alison, and Johnny

> **Explanation:** "You" refers to the named insured. Alison and Johnny are insureds, but they are not named insureds, nor are they included in the definition of "you."

# Property & Casualty Insurance - Property and Casualty Insurance Basics - Property and Casualty Insurance Policy Conditions

### Question 1
**If the insurer and the insured cannot agree on the value of damaged property in settling a property loss claim, all the following statements are correct EXCEPT:**

- [ ] a. The insurance company is required to select an appraiser.
- [ ] b. The insured is required to select an appraiser.
- [ ] c. The insured's appraiser and the insurance company's appraiser are required to select a third appraiser to act as umpire.
- [x] **d. The umpire appraiser's valuation must be accepted by all parties.**

> **Explanation:** An agreement on the loss valuation by any two of the three appraisers establishes the value of the loss.

---

### Question 2
**Because he was watching his GPS instead of watching his driving, Gerry's car rear-ended the vehicle in front of him. It will cost $15,000 to repair that vehicle, and the two injured occupants of that vehicle are each awarded $30,000 in damages. Gerry's auto policy has combined single limits of $50,000. How much will his insurer pay?**

- [ ] a. $15,000
- [ ] b. $45,000
- [x] **c. $50,000**
- [ ] d. $65,000

> **Explanation:** Although total damages total $75,000, the insurer's obligations are capped by the $50,000 limit Gerry chose. Gerry will have to make up the $25,000 difference with his own funds.

---

### Question 3
**Randi's professional liability policy has a one-year term and a $500,000 aggregate limit. This means that the insurer will pay no more than $500,000 for:**

- [ ] a. all claims arising out of one incident
- [x] **b. all claims during the one-year policy period**
- [ ] c. all claims made by a single claimant before or during the policy year
- [ ] d. all claims under the policy since it was issued

> **Explanation:** An aggregate limit is the maximum amount the insurer will pay for all applicable claims during the policy period.

---

### Question 4
**Harriet's dental office sustained serious water damage when a pipe broke sometime over the weekend. Which one of the following is NOT a duty required of Harriet by her property insurance policy?**

- [ ] a. Cooperate with the insurer in the investigation.
- [x] **b. Notify the police.**
- [ ] c. Provide a sworn proof of loss if the insurer requests it.
- [ ] d. Provide prompt notice of the claim to the insurer or its agent.

> **Explanation:** Harriet is required to notify the police only if a crime has been committed. Unless evidence suggests that somebody caused the pipe to break, the loss involves no crime, and there is no need to notify the police.

# Property & Casualty Insurance - Property and Casualty Insurance Basics - Insurance Regulation

### Question 1
**What is the purpose of the notice to consumers required by the Fair Credit Reporting Act?**

- [ ] a. to advise that consumer information in an application may be used as evidence in court
- [x] **b. to advise that the insurer may order a credit report on the applicant that includes personal information**
- [ ] c. to confirm that an insurer's privacy policy is legal
- [ ] d. to give the opportunity to opt out of a credit investigation

> **Explanation:** The written notice required under the FCRA advises applicants of their rights with regard to a credit report that the insurer may use when underwriting an insurance application.

---

### Question 2
**Which federal law prevents certain convicted felons from engaging in the insurance business unless they obtain a waiver from the Department of Insurance?**

- [ ] a. Federal Trade Commission Act (FTCA)
- [ ] b. McCarran-Ferguson Act
- [ ] c. Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA)
- [x] **d. Violent Crime Control and Law Enforcement Act**

> **Explanation:** The Violent Crime Control and Law Enforcement Act makes it a criminal offense for a person convicted of a felony involving breach of trust or dishonesty to participate in the business of insurance without first obtaining a Fraud and False Statements 1033 waiver from a state insurance regulatory official.

---

### Question 3
**The Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA) does NOT compel insurance companies to offer coverage for certified acts of terrorism in which of the following lines of insurance?**

- [ ] a. commercial liability
- [ ] b. commercial property
- [x] **c. homeowners**
- [ ] d. workers compensation

> **Explanation:** TRIA requires that property and casualty insurers selling insurance in the United States make terrorism coverage available under commercial property, commercial liability, and workers compensation insurance policies. There is no similar requirement with respect to homeowners policies.

---

- [ ] c. The insurer's privacy policy complies with legal requirements.
- [ ] d. They are entitled to opt out of a credit investigation.
