# CA Property and Casualty Agent Exam - California General Insurance Laws - Basic Insurance Concepts and Principles

## Questions and Answers

### 1. In California, which one of the following does not qualify as insurance?

* **a.** Casandra's homeowners policy
* **b. Debbie's agreement to regularly clean a dining hall**
* **c.** Kelly's auto insurance policy
* **d.** Mason's workers compensation insurance

> **Explanation:** Debbie's agreement to regularly clean a dining hall is not a promise to pay for loss or damage arising from an uncertain event. Under California law, insurance requires a contract to indemnify another against loss, damage, or liability arising from a contingent or unknown event.

---

### 2. Wilmer's auto insurance paid $2,000 of the $2,500 it will cost to repair the collision damage to Wilmer's car. It did not pay the remaining $500 because Wilmer's policy has a(n)

* **a.** $2,000 limit.
* **b.** Coinsurance clause.
* **c. Deductible.**
* **d.** Self-funding clause.

> **Explanation:** Wilmer's policy has a $500 collision deductible, which means the insurer will not pay $500 of a collision loss. A deductible is the amount of loss paid by the insured before the insurance policy pays.

---

### 3. Collette lives in a home on the ocean that is insured for $20 million. Although her insurance company would not be able to cover a $20 million loss with its own resources, the insurer is able to write a single policy with a $20 million limit for Collette because the insurance company has

* **a.** A large deductible in its policy.
* **b.** Competition from the California Department of Insurance.
* **c. Reinsurance.**
* **d.** Secured credit.

> **Explanation:** By purchasing reinsurance, the insurer transfers some of its risk to a reinsurance company, and that makes it possible for the insurer to handle this large account without over-exposing its own surplus.

---

### 4. Which of the following is NOT a cost associated with operating an insurance system?

* **a.** Cost of capital investment
* **b.** Cost of labor
* **c.** Increased losses due to attitudinal hazards
* **d. Reduced crime rates**

> **Explanation:** The costs associated with operating an insurance system include land, capital investment, labor, administrative expenses, and increased losses due to attitudinal (morale) and moral hazards. Reduced crime rates are a potential societal benefit, not an operational cost of the insurance system.

# CA Property and Casualty Agent Exam - California General Insurance Laws - The Insurance Marketplace

## Questions and Answers

### 1. Georgina's insurance company notifies her that it will not renew her current policy when it expires because her property no longer meets the insurer's underwriting criteria. It appears that Georgina's insurer is engaging in a practice known as

* **a. Post-selection.**
* **b.** Pre-selection.
* **c.** Pretext interview.
* **d.** Unfair discrimination.

> **Explanation:** Reviewing existing insurance contracts like Georgina's and nonrenewing coverage on policyholders who are no longer found to be acceptable is an example of post-selection.

---

### 2. As a recently licensed California insurance agent, Alonzo realizes that he could make a mistake with an insurance policy that might cause an angry client to sue him. Therefore, Alonzo realizes it is important for him to carry

* **a.** A businessowners policy.
* **b.** A crime insurance policy.
* **c. Errors and omissions insurance.**
* **d.** Life insurance.

> **Explanation:** Alonzo should carry errors and omissions (E&O) insurance to protect himself from liability that might arise because he fails to fulfill his duties to either his customers or the insurer(s) he represents.

---

### 3. Three different insurance companies licensed in California have rejected Ferris's application for liability insurance on his amusement park. However, Ferris might still be able to get the coverage he needs from a(n)

* **a.** Alien insurer.
* **b.** Domestic insurer.
* **c.** Foreign insurer.
* **d. Nonadmitted insurer.**

> **Explanation:** A California consumer is legally permitted to buy excess and surplus lines insurance from a nonadmitted insurer, who might or might not be domiciled in California, when the desired coverage is not available from an admitted insurer.

---

### 4. Colossal Casualty Insurance Company, an admitted insurance company in California, has appointed Winfred, who has a California insurance agent's license, to sell Colossal Casualty insurance policies. When he is engaged in providing insurance for Justine, Winfred represents

* **a.** Both Colossal Casualty and Justine.
* **b. Colossal Casualty.**
* **c.** Justine.
* **d.** The California Department of Insurance.

> **Explanation:** A licensed insurance agent (Winfred) represents the admitted insurance company (Colossal Casualty) that has formally appointed him as its agent.

# CA Property and Casualty Agent Exam - California General Insurance Laws - State Insurance Regulation and Contract Law

## Questions and Answers

### 1. California regulates the insurance business within its state lines. However, the federal government regulates insurance business that is associated with

* **a.** fiduciary bonds
* **b.** inland marine risks
* **c. interstate commerce**
* **d.** professional liability

> **Explanation:** While the state principally regulates insurance business within its boundaries or transacted by its licensees, the business comes under federal jurisdiction when it affects or is associated with interstate commerce.

---

### 2. The parties to an insurance contract include the

* **a.** insurer and beneficiary
* **b.** insurer and claimant
* **c. insurer and policyowner**
* **d.** insurer, policyowner, and beneficiary

> **Explanation:** An insurance contract is an agreement between the insurer and the policyowner. Neither the beneficiary nor the claimant is a party to the contract.

---

### 3. Which of these is NOT considered a fraudulent act with respect to insurance transactions in California?

* **a.** knowingly exaggerating facts about a loss to obtain a larger claim settlement
* **b.** materially misrepresenting facts to discourage a party from exercising its rights
* **c. paying a claim before the insurer has completed its investigation of the loss**
* **d.** withholding information from an insured that would increase the amount of the claim settlement

> **Explanation:** An insurer that pays a claim before completing its investigation of the loss does not commit fraud. The other listed actions involve deceit or misrepresentation, which constitute fraudulent acts.

---

### 4. An advantage that an admitted insurer has over an unadmitted insurer in California is the ability to

* **a.** comply with state laws and regulations
* **b.** license its agents in California
* **c.** settle claims from California residents
* **d. transact insurance business in California**

> **Explanation:** An admitted insurer is entitled and certified to transact insurance business in California. Unadmitted insurers can generally only transact business through licensed surplus lines brokers under specific circumstances.

# CA Property and Casualty Agent Exam - California General Insurance Laws - California Privacy Laws

### 1. Rights Under the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)

**Question:**
The California Consumer Privacy Act of 2018 (CCPA) gives California residents the right to do all of the following **EXCEPT**:

* a. non-discrimination for exercising their CCPA rights
* b. opt-out of the sale of personal information
* **c. request that a business delete all personal information ever collected from the consumer**
* d. request that the business disclose what personal information they have collected, used, or shared

**Correct Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:**
Consumers have the right to request that businesses delete their personal information, but this right is **not absolute**. Numerous statutory exceptions allow businesses to retain customer data—such as completing transactions, fulfilling contract obligations, detecting security incidents, or complying with legal requirements. Consumers *do* have absolute rights to opt-out of data sales, request disclosures of collected data, and exercise these rights without facing discrimination.

---

### 2. Definition of Personal Information Under CCPA

**Question:**
Which of the following would **NOT** be considered personal information under the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)?

* a. fingerprints
* b. internet browsing history
* **c. real estate license number**
* d. receipts of products purchased

**Correct Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:**
Fingerprints (biometric data), records of products purchased (commercial information), and internet browsing history (internet activity) are explicitly categorized as personal information under the CCPA. Publicly available government records and professional license information (such as a real estate license number) are generally excluded from the definition of personal information.

---

### 3. Provisions of California's "Shine the Light" Law

**Question:**
All of the following are true of California's "Shine the Light" Law **EXCEPT**:

* a. Businesses with less than 20 employees are exempt
* b. The for-profit business does not have to be located in California but must have a customer who resides in California.
* **c. The for-profit business must be located in California and have customers who reside in California.**
* d. The law requires businesses to establish a designated contact point.

**Correct Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:**
To be subject to the "Shine the Light" law, a business **does not** need to be physically located in California. It applies to any business that has an established business relationship with a California resident and shares personal information for direct marketing purposes. The law exempts businesses with fewer than 20 employees and requires covered entities to maintain a designated contact point for consumer inquiries.

---

### 4. Applicability of California's "Shine the Light" Law

**Question:**
California's "Shine the Light" Law would apply to which of the following businesses?

* **a. an advertising firm with 55 employees**
* b. California non-profit organization
* c. credit reporting bureau
* d. credit union

**Correct Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:**
The "Shine the Light" law applies to for-profit businesses that have 20 or more employees. Statutory exemptions apply to non-profit organizations, credit reporting bureaus, financial institutions subject to specific federal regulations (such as credit unions), political groups, and providers of public real estate records. An advertising firm with 55 employees meets the criteria and is not exempt.
