
# Property & Casualty Insurance - California Casualty Insurance Laws - Casualty Insurance Basics

### Question 1
**While driving, Tyler is distracted by a cellphone call and rear-ends Bennett's car that had stopped for a red light, injuring Bennett's passenger. The passenger's liability claim against Tyler is referred to as a(n):**

- [ ] a. First-party claim.
- [ ] b. Fourth-party claim.
- [ ] c. Second-party claim.
- [x] **d. Third-party claim.**

> **Explanation:** A claim by someone who is not a party to the insurance contract is a third-party claim.

---

### Question 2
**Shauna's insurance premium is lower than Louise's, because Shauna has had no tickets or accidents during the past three years. Louise has a much worse driving record. Shauna's premium is lower because her insurer uses what rating system?**

- [ ] a. Judgment rating
- [ ] b. Manual rating
- [x] **c. Merit rating**
- [ ] d. Open rating

> **Explanation:** Merit rating begins with manual rates but then adjusts them downward for individuals like Shauna who have had good loss experience and upward for individuals like Louise.

---

### Question 3
**In deciding what insurance his company should buy, Ward bases his decisions on the company's past loss history. What is the biggest disadvantage of this approach?**

- [x] **a. It overlooks the types of losses that have not happened yet but could happen in the future.**
- [ ] b. Loss records often contain errors.
- [ ] c. Losses rarely repeat themselves.
- [ ] d. Similar losses could occur in the future.

> **Explanation:** Analyzing past lost records does nothing to recognize the many types of losses that could occur in the future but never occurred before.

---

### Question 4
**Estelle paid the full annual premium when she purchased her policy. But when Estelle canceled her one-year insurance policy after it had been in effect for exactly three months, her insurance agent told her the return premium would be calculated on a short-rate basis. This means that:**

- [ ] a. Estelle will get back exactly three-fourths of the annual premium.
- [x] **b. Estelle will get back less than three-fourths of the annual premium.**
- [ ] c. Estelle will get back more than three-fourths of the annual premium.
- [ ] d. Estelle will not get back any of her premium.

> **Explanation:** When a policy is canceled on a short-rate basis, the insurer is entitled to retain a greater percentage of the unearned premium, and return less of the unearned premium, than would otherwise apply with a pro rata cancellation.

# Property & Casualty Insurance - California Casualty Insurance Laws - Casualty Insurance Policies

### Question 1
**Because mold has become a problem for insurers, many policies now provide limited coverage by:**

- [ ] a. Completely excluding all coverage for microbial matter while continuing to cover loss by other causes.
- [ ] b. Excluding coverage for water damage.
- [x] **c. Excluding mold and then adding an additional coverage with a dollar limit.**
- [ ] d. Limiting coverage for water damage to a stated dollar amount.

> **Explanation:** Many policies now exclude coverage for mold and other microbial matter, unless it results from fire or lightning, and then add an additional coverage with a dollar limit.

---

### Question 2
**Estelle transferred the legal title to her home to her son Jimmie. However, she cannot transfer her insurance policy to Jimmie without the insurer's permission because of what clause in her policy?**

- [x] **a. Assignment**
- [ ] b. Removal
- [ ] c. Severability
- [ ] d. Subrogation

> **Explanation:** The assignment provision states that a policy cannot be transferred (assigned) from one named insured to another party without the insurer's consent.

---

### Question 3
**Heather has been notified that her insurance company will not issue another policy to her when her current policy expires. In other words, the insurer has decided that it will do what to her policy?**

- [ ] a. Cancel
- [ ] b. Expire
- [x] **c. Nonrenew**
- [ ] d. Terminate

> **Explanation:** A decision not to renew an expiring insurance policy is referred to as nonrenewal.

---

### Question 4
**Dr. Lenard purchased an commercial general liability policy on his office. With his insurer's consent, Dr. Lenard is able to transfer his interest in the policy to Dr. Brown. Which of the following policy provisions applies in this case?**

- [x] **a. Assignment.**
- [ ] b. Concurrent.
- [ ] c. Liberalization.
- [ ] d. Transfer of risk.

> **Explanation:** The assignment provision states that a policy can be transferred (assigned) from one named insured to another party with the insurer's consent.

# Property & Casualty Insurance - California Casualty Insurance Laws - Personal Lines Insurance

### Question 1
**Because Harley's personal auto insurance with his current insurer has been in effect for more than 60 days, the insurer is not permitted to cancel the policy if:**

- [x] **a. Harley develops prostate cancer.**
- [ ] b. Harley fails to pay the premium when due.
- [ ] c. Harley lied on his insurance application.
- [ ] d. Harley's drivers license is suspended.

> **Explanation:** A California amendatory endorsement permits the insurer to cancel a PAP that has been in effect for at least 60 days only for nonpayment, suspension or revocation of a driver's license, or misrepresentation of a material factor.

---

### Question 2
**Gayle, a California resident, is the named insured in an ISO personal auto policy issued by a California insurance company. This policy will protect her if she is driving a private passenger auto in all the following places EXCEPT:**

- [ ] a. Alaska.
- [ ] b. Canada.
- [x] **c. Mexico.**
- [ ] d. Puerto Rico.

> **Explanation:** The PAP's territorial limits include the United States and its territories or possessions, Puerto Rico, and Canada, but not Mexico.

---

### Question 3
**Bernadine used to have a live-in nanny, but now that her children are in school she has no residence employees. With respect to the insurance on her home, the California Insurance Code:**

- [ ] a. Does not require that Bernadine's homeowners policy include workers compensation coverage, because she no longer has employees.
- [x] **b. Requires that Bernadine's current homeowners policy include an endorsement providing workers compensation coverage on residence employees, but no premium for that endorsement will appear in her policy declarations.**
- [ ] c. Requires that Bernadine's current homeowners policy include an endorsement providing workers compensation coverage on residence employees, for which she will pay an additional premium that must be shown in her policy declarations.
- [ ] d. Requires that workers compensation coverage on residence employees be offered as an option, but Bernadine may reject that option.

> **Explanation:** The California Insurance Code requires every California homeowners policy to have the Workers Compensation-Residence Employees (CA) Endorsement, but the cost of this coverage is not to be shown as a separate premium.

---

### Question 4
**In reviewing the declarations of her yacht insurance policy, Aurelia sees that it lists "protection and indemnity coverage," and she asks you to explain this coverage to her. You correctly answer that, in a yacht policy:**

- [ ] a. Protection and indemnity coverage is similar to the uninsured motorists coverage in an auto policy.
- [ ] b. Protection and indemnity coverage will protect Aurelia against property losses by indemnifying her for any damage to the hull.
- [x] **c. Protection and indemnity covers her liability for bodily injury and property damage.**
- [ ] d. Protection and indemnity is the nautical term for workers compensation.

> **Explanation:** P&I covers bodily injury and property damage liability losses the insured is responsible for.

# Property & Casualty Insurance - Practice Quiz & Answer Key (Part 58)

### Question 1
**Unlike a true commercial umbrella policy, an excess liability policy is not designed to:**

- [ ] a. Extend the limits of underlying liability policies.
- [ ] b. Meet the needs of businesses that need higher limits than their primary policies can provide.
- [x] **c. Provide broader coverage for some risks than primary policies provide.**
- [ ] d. Replace primary coverage once the primary policy's aggregate limits have been exhausted.

> **Explanation:** An umbrella is designed to afford broader coverage for some risks than primary policies provide, subject to a retention. The same is not true about an excess policy.

---

### Question 2
**A disgruntled former employee who was recently laid off and has been unable to obtain other employment sues her former supervisor, Maryanne, for wrongful termination. What type of insurance might defend Maryanne and her employer against this claim and pay any damages if the former employee wins the lawsuit?**

- [ ] a. Employers liability insurance
- [x] **b. Employment practices liability insurance**
- [ ] c. Fiduciary liability insurance
- [ ] d. Workers compensation insurance

> **Explanation:** Employment practices liability insurance covers wrongful acts, such as wrongful termination, arising from the employment process.

---

### Question 3
**As the owner of a California business, Nathaniel can buy workers compensation insurance from either a private insurance company or:**

- [ ] a. A monopolistic state fund.
- [ ] b. A risk retention group.
- [ ] c. The California Department of Insurance.
- [x] **d. The California State Compensation Fund.**

> **Explanation:** Monopolistic funds operate in only a few states, none of which is California, and risk retention groups are not permitted to sell workers compensation insurance. The biggest workers compensation insurer in the state, the California State Compensation Fund, is a self-supporting non-profit enterprise; it is not a branch of the state.

---

### Question 4
**Randal's business is covered by an occurrence-basis commercial general liability (CGL) policy with a policy term of January 1 of last year to January 1 of this year and a general aggregate limit of $1,000,000. This means that Randal's insurer will pay:**

- [ ] a. A claim of less than $1 million incurred and paid during last year, and that won't affect the amount of coverage available to pay other claims from last year.
- [ ] b. More than $1 million for all claims incurred during last year.
- [x] **c. No more than $1 million for all claims not involving the products-completed operations hazard incurred during last year.**
- [ ] d. Up to $1 million for every claim incurred during last year.

> **Explanation:** $1 million is the most that Randal's insurer will pay for all bodily injury and property damage claims (except for products-completed operations claims) and all personal and advertising injury claims during the policy period (last year).
