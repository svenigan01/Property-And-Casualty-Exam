
# Property & Casualty Insurance - California Property and Casualty Insurance - Legal Concepts of Tort Law

### Question 1
**Dwayne works for a drug store. He sees Joel, a customer, carrying a large bag and acting suspicious, so Dwayne suspects Joel of shoplifting. Without creating a disturbance, Dwayne quietly asks Joel to go to the store's office where Dwayne keeps him until the police arrived. When they search Joel and his bag, they do not find any stolen merchandise. Joel sues Dwayne and the store, accusing them of:**

- [x] **a. False arrest.**
- [ ] b. Gross negligence.
- [ ] c. Libel.
- [ ] d. Slander.

> **Explanation:** From Joel's perspective, detaining Joel was an unlawful restraint on his freedom. If Dwayne had accused Joel in front of other shoppers, he might also have been sued for slander.

### Question 2
**The Podunk Press published an anonymous letter to the editor in which the writer stated that the world would end on a certain date. That date passed and the world did not end. Although the published statement was false, it is not libel because:**

- [ ] a. In publishing this statement, the newspaper intended no harm.
- [ ] b. Libel consists of spoken false statements, and this statement was delivered in writing.
- [ ] c. Libel is an unintentional tort, and this publication was intentional.
- [x] **d. The statement did not damage a person's reputation.**

> **Explanation:** Libel, an intentional tort, is a written or printed untrue statement that intentionally or unintentionally damages a person's reputation.

### Question 3
**Bertie forgot to erect a warning sign after she mopped the floor in Giovanni's Pizza Parlor while Giovanni was out making a delivery. A patron who failed to notice the wet floor slipped and broke her ankle. Because Bertie is Giovanni's employee, Giovanni may be held responsible for the customer's injuries under the principle of:**

- [ ] a. Errors and omissions.
- [ ] b. Gross negligence.
- [ ] c. Intentional torts.
- [x] **d. Vicarious liability.**

> **Explanation:** An employer may be held responsible for the work-related acts of its employees under the vicarious liability principle.

---

### Question 4
**During a meeting with the company's top executives, Trudy falsely claimed that Joanne had been the subject of an internal investigation at a former employer, though Trudy knew that this was not true. Trudy is guilty of:**

- [ ] a. An accidental tort.
- [x] **b. An intentional tort.**
- [ ] c. Assault.
- [ ] d. Negligence.

> **Explanation:** In making this slanderous statement, Trudy committed an intentional tort.
