# Property & Casualty Insurance Practice Questions

## Questions

### 1.
The concept of indemnity is best illustrated by:

a. Replacing a stolen ten-year-old television set with a new one of same size, make and model  
b. Reducing a claim payment due to a depreciation of damaged property  
c. Applying a coinsurance penalty to a loss  
d. Applying a deductible to a collision loss  

---

### 2.
In auto insurance, insurable interest MUST exist at which of the following times?

a. At each renewal date  
b. Whenever an insurance company requests proof of its existence  
c. At the time of application and loss  
d. At the time a quote is requested  

---

### 3.
Insurance is designed to provide protection against which of the following types of risk?

a. Innovative  
b. Pure  
c. Speculative  
d. Hazard  

---

### 4.
Farmer John's barn was destroyed when a tornado blew down one of its walls. The wall knocked over a lantern, causing a fire that burned down the entire barn. The proximate cause of loss was the:

a. Tornado  
b. Fire  
c. Water damage from the storm  
d. Collapse of the wall  

---

### 5.
The principle of indemnity is designed to avoid:

a. The return of earned premiums  
b. Overpayment on a claim  
c. The non-payment of a claim by an insurer  
d. Pro-rata liability payments  

---

### 6.
The primary responsibility of an insurance company is to:

a. Inspect the insured's property on a regular basis  
b. Provide the insured with a safety program to help modify losses  
c. Provide inventory sheets to the insured along with the policy  
d. Pay the claims covered under the policy  

---

### 7.
Frank sold his car to Jill but did not cancel his auto insurance policy. Jill had an accident one month later, and Frank reported the accident to his insurance company.

Frank was not able to collect money for damages to the car, even though his policy was still in force because:

a. Frank was not the individual who had the accident  
b. The insurance company did not follow proper claims procedure  
c. Frank misrepresented that he still owned the vehicle by keeping the policy in force  
d. Frank no longer had insurable interest  

---

### 8.
Which of the following is NOT an example of insurable interest?

a. An individual's interest in property their parents told them they have left to them in a will  
b. A tenant's interest in improvements they have made to a rental building  
c. A cleaner's interest in a customer's clothing that is still at the cleaner's facility  
d. An individual's interest in property being bought through a loan  

---

# Answers and Explanations

### 1. Answer: B

**Explanation:**  
Indemnity means restoring the insured to approximately the same financial position they were in before the loss—no better and no worse. Reducing a claim payment due to depreciation reflects actual cash value and supports indemnity.

*Why not A?* Replacing a 10-year-old TV with a brand-new one may leave the insured in a better position than before the loss.

---

### 2. Answer: C

**Explanation:**  
For auto insurance, insurable interest must exist at the time the policy is purchased and at the time of loss. The insured must stand to suffer a financial loss if the property is damaged.

---

### 3. Answer: B

**Explanation:**  
Insurance is intended to cover **pure risks**, which involve the possibility of loss only (e.g., fire, theft, accident).

*Speculative risks* involve the possibility of gain or loss (e.g., investing in stocks) and are generally not insurable.

---

### 4. Answer: A

**Explanation:**  
The tornado set into motion the chain of events that ultimately destroyed the barn. Under the doctrine of proximate cause, the original peril that starts an uninterrupted sequence of events is considered the proximate cause.

Tornado → Wall Collapse → Lantern Falls → Fire → Barn Destroyed

Therefore, the proximate cause is **the tornado**.

---

### 5. Answer: B

**Explanation:**  
The principle of indemnity prevents the insured from profiting from a loss. Its purpose is to avoid overpayment and ensure that claim payments only compensate for the actual financial loss.

---

### 6. Answer: D

**Explanation:**  
Although insurers may provide inspections, safety recommendations, and other services, their primary obligation under the insurance contract is to pay covered claims according to policy terms.

---

### 7. Answer: D

**Explanation:**  
Once Frank sold the car, he no longer had a financial interest in it. Because he no longer owned the vehicle, he could not suffer a loss from damage to it.

Without insurable interest at the time of loss, he cannot collect under the policy.

---

### 8. Answer: A

**Explanation:**  
A future inheritance is only an expectation and does not create a current financial interest in the property.

The other choices represent valid insurable interests because the individuals could suffer a financial loss if the property were damaged.

- Tenant improvements = financial investment
- Cleaner's custody of customer property = potential liability
- Property purchased with a loan = ownership interest

Therefore, **A** is not an insurable interest.
