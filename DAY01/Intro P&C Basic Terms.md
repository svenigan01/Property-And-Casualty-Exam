# Types of Losses

## 1. Direct Loss

A **direct loss** is the immediate physical damage caused by a covered peril.

### Example
A hailstorm damages the roof of a home.

- Hail damage to the roof = **Direct Physical Loss**

---

## 2. Indirect (Consequential) Loss

An **indirect loss** results from a direct loss and occurs as a consequence of the original damage.

### Example
A hailstorm damages a home's roof, making the home temporarily uninhabitable.

- Hail damage to the roof = **Direct Loss**
- Hotel expenses while repairs are made = **Indirect (Consequential) Loss**

---

## Intervening Cause

An **intervening cause** is an event that interrupts the chain of causation between the original event and the final loss.

### Example
You are involved in an automobile accident and are waiting for the police to arrive. Before the vehicle can be moved, a tree falls on the car and causes additional damage.

- Auto accident = Original event
- Falling tree = Intervening cause

---

## Proximate Cause

The **proximate cause** is the primary cause that sets a chain of events into motion and results in a loss.

### Example
Lightning strikes a house and breaks a window.

- Lightning = Proximate cause
- Broken window = Resulting damage

### 5.
Which of the following is considered a hazard?

a. A fire  
b. A wet floor in a store  
c. A burned-down building  
d. An insurance policy  

---

# Answers and Explanations

### 1. Answer: C — Indemnity

**Explanation:**  
The principle of indemnity is designed to restore an insured to approximately the same financial position they occupied before a loss. It prevents the insured from profiting from an insurance claim.

---

### 2. Answer: C — They have additional rights and responsibilities under the policy

**Explanation:**  
The First Named Insured has certain policy privileges and responsibilities that other insureds may not have, such as:

- Receiving policy notices
- Requesting policy changes
- Canceling the policy
- Paying premiums

They are not necessarily the only person covered under the policy.

---

### 3. Answer: B — A principle that allows insurers to predict losses more accurately

**Explanation:**  
The Law of Large Numbers states that as the number of similar exposure units increases, insurers can more accurately predict future losses.

This principle is one of the foundations of insurance and rate-making.

---

### 4. Answer: D — A sudden, unexpected event causing loss or damage

**Explanation:**  
In insurance, an accident is generally defined as an unforeseen and unintended event that results in injury, damage, or loss.

Examples include:
- Automobile collisions
- Slip-and-fall incidents
- Accidental fires

---

### 5. Answer: B — A wet floor in a store

**Explanation:**  
A hazard is a condition that increases the likelihood or severity of a loss.

A wet floor increases the chance that someone may slip and be injured.

**Why the others are incorrect:**
- Fire = Peril (cause of loss)
- Burned-down building = Loss
- Insurance policy = Contract

Therefore, a wet floor is the best example of a hazard.
